# How to use your brain

Ready-made sentences. Copy, paste, rewrite in your own words. You don't have to learn them by heart — this file exists so you don't have to.

---

## Every time you start and finish

> **Finish by saving.**

**At the start** you don't have to do anything. In the browser, every new chat starts from the latest version on GitHub.

**At the end:**
> *Save all changes to the main version on GitHub.*

Or shorter: *"push to main"* or *"save to my brain"*. All three mean the same thing.

**If you're not sure everything is saved:**
> *Are all of today's changes in the main version (`main`)?*

Or open github.com, go to your brain's repo, and check: is the latest change from today?

---

## When you start in a new chat window

Every chat starts cold. The AI doesn't remember last time — the repo carries the context, not the chat. So write to it the way you would to a new employee:

> *Read through my brain. Today we're doing [what you're doing]. Everything you need is in the repo.*

Don't write "like we discussed last time" — for the AI, there was no last time.

---

## The first sentence, while your brain is still empty

> *Read through my brain and tell me what's here and what's still empty.*

---

## When you don't know where something goes

You don't need to know GitHub or the folder rules. Just describe what you have:

> *I have [describe the material]. What do I do with it and where does it go? Don't move anything yet. First give me a safe recommendation, ask only for what's missing, and explain why in plain language.*

For example:

> *I have a folder full of old invoices. What do I do with it and where does it go?*

Your brain should first say that the original invoices stay in the agent account's Drive, not in the brain. Then it asks only:

1. what you want to get out of the invoices;
2. whether the AI may read client data, or whether it has to be anonymised first.

The final answer tells you:

- where to keep the originals;
- what the brain should learn from them;
- why;
- what the next step is.



## Seeding your brain

If the material might contain client data, passwords, banking or health information, **don't give the AI the whole folder to read yet.** Describe the material without attaching any files:

> *I have [describe the material]. It may contain private information. What do I do with it? Don't open or move any files yet.*

Your brain will tell you whether the originals can go into the agent account's Drive, whether anything needs to be anonymised offline first, and what is worth the brain learning from them. You don't have to decide the technical rules of GitHub or AI processing yourself.

Two moves, and most people need both.

### 1. Show it what you already have

**Put the material into one folder in the agent account's Drive** — for example `Raw material`. Your website, old proposals, price list, a ChatGPT export, a voice memo transcript. Then say:

> *Read through the `Raw material` folder in Drive and distil it into my brain. Don't copy the raw material itself into the brain.*

**Point to one specific folder, not your whole Drive.** That way the AI knows exactly what to read.

**Something short you can also paste straight into the chat** or add as a screenshot — your website text, one email, one post.

> You may put a PDF, image, Word file or screenshot into Drive **only if the AI provider is allowed to process its contents.** The fact that a file doesn't go to GitHub doesn't mean the AI isn't processing it when it reads it.

### 2. Let it ask you

This is the part that isn't written down anywhere — your voice, why you turn some clients down, what you would never do.

> *You've read what I have. Now ask me what's missing — one or two questions at a time — and write my answers into the right file. Look at `TOPICS.md` for where to start.*

**The order matters: first the material, even a little, and only then the interview.** An AI that has read your website asks *"your website shows three packages, but your invoices show only one — which is true right now?"*. A cold AI asks *"describe your business"*. Same tool, a completely different question.

## Brain + Drive — you don't have to move everything into your brain

Invoices, client register, client folders, images — these are not, and don't need to be, part of your brain. **They live in the agent account's Drive.** The AI reads them through a connector when the work needs it.

> *Make a new invoice. Take the general rules from my brain; take the client's details and previous invoices from the `Invoices` folder in Drive. Save the draft in that same Drive folder, not in my brain.*

> *Add yesterday's new client to the client register in Drive — check my brain for who they are and what they want.*

What's happening here:

- **Your brain gives the context** — who the client is, what your prices are, how you phrase things.
- **Drive is the desk** — the samples come from there, the result goes there.
- **Drive doesn't become part of your brain.** Nothing gets copied to GitHub.

Only what you say should go into your brain goes into it:

> *Don't put these invoices into my brain. Put into my brain only that this client's price goes up from 1 September.*

**That's why you lose nothing by keeping client data out of your brain.** It's in Drive, one click away, and your brain knows exactly as much about it as you want it to.

> **The AI can't see your computer.** An AI working in the browser sees only your brain (GitHub) and what is in the agent account's Drive. If a file is only on your computer, upload it to Drive. Connecting your computer itself to the AI is a later, separate decision — do a risk assessment first.

> **If the AI says it can't save a file to Drive**, ask it to give you the file to download and drag it into Drive yourself. The AI can usually create text files and Google Docs itself; bigger files (PDFs, slides) not always.

## Skills

A skill is a guide for one recurring job. Your skills are in the `skills/` folder, and the list of them is in `AGENTS.md`.

**What skills do I have?**
> *What skills are in my brain and when do I use them?*

**Use a skill:**
> *Do a risk assessment.*

**Make a new skill** — when you've done some job with the AI and want it to go the same way next time:
> *Turn what we just did into a skill. Put it in the `skills/` folder and add it to the table in `AGENTS.md`.*

## Ask your brain yourself

**This is the most important sentence of week 1.** Use it once there's already something in your brain.

> *Read through my whole brain and be honest with me.*
>
> *1. What contradicts itself here — where do I say one thing and do another?*
> *2. What's missing — what should any AI know about my business that it can't find here?*
> *3. What have I probably not thought about?*
> *4. What are the three things holding back my growth the most right now?*
> *5. What is the one job you would take off my hands first?*

> **This works really well once your brain already knows you and your business.** If the answer comes back thin, that's not a sign it isn't working — it's a sign your brain is still hungry. Keep feeding it and ask again in a week.

---

## Daily rhythm

**First thing in the morning:**
> *Read through my brain. Today I'm doing [...]. What do you think is the most important thing today?*

**At the end of the day:**
> *Save what matters to my brain: [what happened, what was decided, what's next]. Write it into the right file and save it to the main version on GitHub.*

---

## When something went well

> *Add this to my evidence list in `01-me/future-me.md`.*

---

## Don't trust blindly

When your AI suggests or asks something and you don't understand why:

> *Explain to me why this matters.*

AI amplifies wisdom, and foolishness too. You are the one who decides.
