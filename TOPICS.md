# What your brain wants to know about you

You don't have to fill all of this in at once. **You don't even have to fill it in on your own** — your brain will keep asking for more as it goes, whenever something is missing.

Here are the topics. Each one says what you could give the AI: links, files, screenshots — or just talk and let the AI write.

**Start with the first two.** After that it's up to you, at your own pace — all at once in one afternoon, or a little every day. In practice, the rest fills up during the week anyway, as you work.

---

## 1. What your goals are and how to get there

→ `CONTEXT.md` (goal) and `05-decisions/my-numbers.md` (numbers)

Where you want to be a year from now, and what this month's goal is. And your numbers right now. And one number most people have never worked out: **how many clients can you actually serve?**

**What to bring:** revenue, clients, working hours · what an average client pays you · how many clients you can handle at once right now · as precisely as you know

> It's completely okay if you can't answer everything right now.

## 2. Your ideal client

→ `02-offer/ideal-client.md`

Describe the clients who bring you the most joy and also the most money. And — just as important — the ones you no longer want to work with.

**What to bring:** a client list or a few recent projects · a client's website that shows what they do · feedback and thank-you notes, **in the clients' own words** · email threads where a client asked questions or hesitated before buying — **that's where the typical objections come from, the ones your sales copy will later need to answer** · how they find you.

> The question "who don't you want to work with" is there so we learn to spot them. Ideal client information is needed to steer your marketing and sales.

## 3. Your style and voice

→ `01-me/style-and-voice.md`

This is the style that runs through how you talk to clients, in sales and in marketing. When your brain knows it, everything it writes for you sounds like you — not like AI.

**What to bring:** a link to your website · screenshots of designed materials · a brand style guide or guidelines, if you have them · 2–3 things you've written yourself (a post, a client email, a proposal) · your LinkedIn profile

> Three real texts teach your voice more than half an hour of describing it.

## 4. What you sell

→ `02-offer/services.md` and `02-offer/pricing.md`

Your services, packages, prices. Including the prices that aren't on your website.

**What to bring:** price list · proposal files · recent invoices · the services page of your website

## 5. How you work

→ `03-processes/client-journey.md`, `03-processes/what-repeats.md` and `03-processes/templates.md`

What happens when a client says yes — step by step, until the work is done. And what repeats in exactly the same way with every client.

**What to bring:** existing templates, checklists, contract templates · a few real documents you've sent out (a proposal, a summary, an invoice) · a description in your own words

> **Documents you make again every time are worth turning into templates** — take the client's details out, keep the structure. See [`03-processes/templates.md`](03-processes/templates.md). Your brain will then know which documents your business uses and can make the next one itself.
>
> This is where you'll find the thing you hand off first in week 4.

### And where information reaches you

The other half of the same question, and nobody asks it of themselves. **Where does information about your business actually come into being?**

Calls and meetings. Email. LinkedIn messages. Messenger. Invoices. CRM software. Notes on your phone.

Just make a list of them and mark two things next to each place: **what's valuable there**, and **whether it reaches your brain right now or not.**

**What to bring:** just talk

> Most of these places will never reach your brain, and at first that's completely okay. But none of them can be automated before you've written them down. The list itself is half the work: it usually turns out that one or two places hold the most valuable information, and those are exactly the ones nobody has ever gathered anywhere.

## 6. What value do you create for your client? What's the proof?

→ `02-offer/client-results.md`

Not what you do, but what is different for the client afterwards. And the proof: your clients' success stories.

**What to bring:** client success stories · results and numbers, if you have them · before-and-after examples · feedback and recommendations · how long it usually takes

> Without this, your AI writes about what you do — "interior design service" instead of "the flat sold three weeks faster".

## 7. Where clients come from — and where things get stuck right now

→ `04-marketing/channels.md` (channels) and `CONTEXT.md` (bottleneck)

Two things that together tell your brain what to recommend to you at all.

**Where they come from:** referrals, website, social media, direct outreach, ads — and roughly how many from where. Most people really have only one channel, but nobody has said it out loud.

**Where it gets stuck:** do you have too few interested people? Or there are interested people, but no sales calls come of it? Or you have clients, but you can't keep up with serving them? Or you can, but the profit is small?

**What to bring:** just talk

> This is the most important field in your whole brain. A brain that doesn't know it recommends content marketing when in fact you already have more clients than you can serve. A brain that knows will tell you instead what you should hand off.
>
> And it changes. When the bottleneck moves, write over it — that's the whole point.

## 8. What has already been tried

→ `05-decisions/already-tried.md`

What didn't work — marketing, services, tools, collaborations. And what has already been decided and doesn't need revisiting.

**What to bring:** just talk

> This is the topic nobody thinks to put into their brain — and it's exactly what stops your AI from suggesting, every month, something you've already tried.

## 9. Future me *(optional)*

→ `01-me/future-me.md`

Who you are once all of this has started working. And what you tell yourself when you get stuck — the sentences that stop you.

**What to bring:** only yourself

## How it actually fills up

You don't sit down and fill your whole brain with information. **You do your normal work together with your brain, and your brain asks for what's missing.**

For example: you ask it to put together a proposal for a client. Your brain sees it doesn't have your prices, and asks. You answer. It writes that into the right file and finishes the proposal. Next time it doesn't ask.

A week later your brain holds information that gathered there naturally while you worked. Without you filling in long forms.
